# Camera, lens, light and colour

Words the video and image models follow. Name the camera move first in the prompt, one move per shot.

## Shot sizes
| Size | Shows | Use it for |
|---|---|---|
| Extreme wide | the place, people tiny | opening, scale, ending |
| Wide | the whole scene | where we are, who is where |
| Full | one person head to toe | movement, costume |
| Medium | waist up | talking, actions with hands |
| Medium close-up | chest up | most dialogue |
| Close-up | the face | feeling, a reaction |
| Extreme close-up | eyes, a hand, an object | a detail that matters |
| Insert | an object in the action | the letter, a key, a screen |
| Over the shoulder | one person past another's shoulder | conversations |

## Camera moves
Static ("locked-off camera"), slow dolly in, dolly out, pan left / right, tilt up / down, tracking shot following
from the side or behind, crane up / down, handheld with slight shake, slow orbit, slow zoom. Say the speed. Say what
the camera starts and ends on when it matters ("tilt up from her shoes to her face"). The subject's move and the
camera's move are separate sentences. To land a move exactly, fix the frames: LTX Frames (At, Strength, Binding) or
MiniMax H3 First frame and Last frame.

## Coverage and continuity
- Coverage for a dialogue scene: a wide (master) that shows both, then over-the-shoulder or medium close-ups of each,
  then the reactions. More choices in the edit.
- The 180-degree rule: draw a line between the two people; keep the camera on one side for the whole scene. Then
  @june always looks screen right and @stranger screen left. Write it in the prompts: "@june on the left, facing
  right".
- Eyelines: in singles, each looks just off camera toward where the other is ("looking off camera to the right").
- Screen direction: someone who walks out to the right enters the next shot from the left.
- Cut on action: end a shot mid-move and start the next on the same move.
- Keep the light direction the same within a scene ("warm light from the left").

## Lens words
- 24mm or wider: wide, depth, a little distortion; places, movement.
- 35mm: natural, documentary.
- 50mm: close to the eye; most shots.
- 85mm: portrait, soft background; close-ups.
- Shallow depth of field, soft background bokeh; or deep focus, everything sharp.
- Anamorphic: wide, oval bokeh, lens flares; a "movie" feel.

## Light words
Soft window light; hard sunlight; golden hour, warm low sun; blue hour; overcast, flat; practical lights (lamps,
neon, a fridge glow); fluorescent overhead; candlelight; moonlight; rim light, backlight; high-key (bright, few
shadows); low-key (dark, deep shadows); chiaroscuro; volumetric light through haze; neon reflections on wet streets.

## Colour and film
Teal and orange; warm amber; cold blue; desaturated, muted; rich saturated; bleach bypass; 35mm film grain; Kodak
Portra tones; 16mm, grainy; black and white, high contrast. Choose one palette for the film and save it as a look.

## Sound in the prompt
Both video families make sound. Say what we hear: room tone, rain on glass, distant traffic, a fridge hum, footsteps
on tile, wind. Keep music out of the shots; it fights the cut.
